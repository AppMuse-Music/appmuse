Anime & Short-Film Scoring

Original scores for anime, animated shorts and live-action short films: music that poeticises a story rather than merely accompanying it.

My Philosophy

Anime taught me that music can make the unspeakable visible. The moment a character understands something they can never undo: that is where a score must already be waiting. Not underlining what the image says. Arriving before it.

For short films, every second of picture carries disproportionate weight. I work from the director's vision, building themes that develop across the arc, honouring the intimacy of the form rather than inflating it.

My work draws on classical training from Paris conservatories, deep engagement with Japanese aesthetic sensibility and Nordic restraint. The beauty found in transience; the depth beneath stillness. A score can hold both the grandeur of an isekai climax and the silence after a quiet, devastating revelation.

Emotional Storytelling

Every theme is composed as a character's inner life made audible. Motifs that carry weight, develop with the arc, and leave a resonance that outlasts the final frame. The score is not decoration. It is part of the structure.

🎹

Piano as the Narrative Voice

Piano is at the heart of each score: intimate where intimacy is needed, expansive at the climax, silent in precisely the right moments. It is the instrument closest to breath, and the one most capable of carrying what a character cannot say.

🗾

Japanese Aesthetic Roots

Drawing on anison sensibility, classical counterpoint and the contemplative tradition of Japanese art music, these are scores that feel authentically Eastern in spirit without superficial execution. The quiet ache of something irretrievably lost. Not a decorative idea, but a compositional tool.

🎬

Short-Film Precision

Short films demand economy. Every cue must earn its place. I compose to picture, synchronising with editorial rhythm and working collaboratively with directors from first sketch to final mix.

Scoring to Raw Animation

Animatics, storyboards, unfinished cuts: scored with full intention

In animation, the score often precedes the finished picture. Pipelines are long and animatics are rough. This is not a limitation. It is where compositional judgement becomes most visible.

Working from raw material means reading intent from pacing, dialogue rhythm and sparse movement, then composing towards an emotion the image has not yet delivered. It asks more of the imagination than a polished cut, not less. It is also how the professional pipeline works: music is committed before the final renders, so the score shapes the edit rather than following it.

A locked animatic is enough to begin thematic development. An early storyboard is enough to establish the emotional palette and the first character motifs. Music entrusted to a film this early has time to become structural rather than ornamental. What follows was composed in precisely this manner.
